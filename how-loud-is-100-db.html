How loud is 100 dB?

100 dB is extremely loud. Approximate comparisons include a loud concert or nightclub, a motorcycle at full throttle nearby, a jackhammer, or headphones near maximum volume. NIDCD lists music through headphones at maximum volume, sporting events, and concerts at about 94–110 dBA, and lawn mowers at about 80–100 dBA. NIOSH says that at 95 dBA or more you will likely have to shout to be heard by someone about 1 meter away.

What about 100 dB sounds like

Compared with 90 dB, 100 dB has about ten times the sound energy and is usually heard as about twice as loud.

Under the NIOSH rule (85 dBA for 8 hours, time halved every 3 dBA), 100 dBA is 15 dB above the limit: five halvings, so 8 hours becomes 15 minutes a day. WHO's environmental noise guidance also recommends that short-term average exposure from occasional leisure noise stay at or below 100 dB LAeq over 15 minutes.

At this level a phone microphone may read low because of compression. Assume the real level could be higher, and do not stay in it to 'get a better sample'.

Hearing

About 15 minutes a day is the NIOSH-based limit at 100 dBA. Earplugs, distance from speakers, and quiet time afterward are the practical steps. NIDCD notes that a temporary shift can fade in 16 to 48 hours and that lasting damage can still remain.

Published health guidance is context. It is not a verdict on a recording from a phone.

Measuring with SOUNDTEST.PRO

The browser meter gives an estimate from the microphone in the phone or computer, not a reading from a certified sound level meter. The product's accuracy page describes a typical field spread of about ±1 to 5 dB after the built-in device baseline. Use it to compare times and keep a dated note. A watermarked PDF preview is included; removing the watermark is paid. CSV export of the session figures is available from the meter.
