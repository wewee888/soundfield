How loud is 70 dB?

70 dB is noticeably loud. Approximate comparisons are busy city traffic from the sidewalk, a loud conversation, a washing machine or dishwasher close by, or a television turned up in a small room. NIDCD puts normal conversation at about 60–70 dBA, so 70 dB is the top of that range. Many vacuum cleaners are in the 70s close up. NIOSH also lists some vacuums among equipment that can reach about 85–90 dBA, so the same kind of machine is not one number.

What about 70 dB sounds like

At about 70 dB, conversation takes more effort and the noise becomes tiring. Compared with 60 dB, it has about ten times the energy and is usually heard as about twice as loud.

70 dB(A) is the daytime limit in China's GB 3096-2008 for Class 4a and 4b areas, the land next to the major roads and railways that standard defines. It is well above the WHO Environmental Noise Guidelines (2018) recommendation of 53 dB Lden for road traffic. Those figures answer different questions from a short phone reading.

Hold the phone still, uncovered, and pointed toward the source. Note whether you used A-weighting.

Hearing

NIDCD says sounds at or below 70 dBA are generally safe even with long exposure, so 70 dB is about the upper edge of that statement. NIDCD and NIOSH treat risk as starting with long or repeated exposure at 85 dBA and above.

Published health guidance is context. It is not a verdict on a recording from a phone.

Sources

  • WHO Environmental Noise Guidelines for the European Region (2018)
