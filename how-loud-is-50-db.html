How loud is 50 dB?

50 dB is a moderate background. Familiar approximate comparisons include a quiet office, moderate rainfall, a quiet conversation at home, or light traffic heard from a distance. It is usually easy to talk over, and you notice it if you are trying to concentrate or sleep.

What about 50 dB sounds like

Indoors at night, about 50 dB is loud enough to disturb sleep for many people. Outdoors in the daytime it is a typical level in a quiet residential area. These comparisons are approximate.

WHO's 1999 Guidelines for Community Noise say that, to protect most people from being moderately annoyed in the daytime, outdoor sound in living areas should not exceed 50 dB LAeq. China's GB 3096-2008 uses 50 dB(A) as the daytime limit for Class 0 areas and the night limit for Class 2 mixed areas. A log of LAeq and L10 over several hours is a more useful supporting record than a single number. Whether anyone accepts that record is up to them.

If a neighbor's music, an air conditioner, or a road brings a room to about 50 dB at night, note the duration and look at LAeq and L10 rather than one peak.

Hearing

50 dB is not a hearing-damage level. NIDCD says sounds at or below 70 dBA are unlikely to cause hearing loss from the exposure itself. Annoyance, and sleep at night, are the issues WHO discusses at this kind of level.

Published health guidance is context. It is not a verdict on a recording from a phone.

Measuring with SOUNDTEST.PRO

The browser meter gives an estimate from the microphone in the phone or computer, not a reading from a certified sound level meter. The product's accuracy page describes a typical field spread of about ±1 to 5 dB after the built-in device baseline. Use it to compare times and keep a dated note. A watermarked PDF preview is included; removing the watermark is paid. CSV export of the session figures is available from the meter.
