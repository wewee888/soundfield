How loud is 60 dB?

60 dB is about the level of normal conversation between two people a meter or so apart. NIDCD lists normal conversation at about 60–70 dBA. Other approximate 60 dB comparisons are a busy restaurant at a moderate time of day, an office with people talking, or background music at home.

What about 60 dB sounds like

Compared with 50 dB, 60 dB has about ten times the sound energy and is usually heard as about twice as loud. You can often still talk without raising your voice. It is clearly not quiet.

For environmental noise, 60 dB is on the high side of daytime guidance. WHO's community-noise guidelines say outdoor daytime levels in living areas should not exceed 55 dB LAeq if the aim is to protect most people from serious annoyance. In China, GB 3096-2008 sets 60 dB(A) as the daytime limit for Class 2 mixed areas and as the night limit for Class 4b areas beside the railway lines the standard names. Indoors at night, a steady 60 dB is far above WHO's 30 dB bedroom guideline and would disturb most people's sleep.

A few minutes of LAeq tells you more than the highest number that flashes on the screen.

Hearing

60 dB is not the level NIDCD treats as a hearing-loss risk. NIDCD says sounds at or below 70 dBA are generally in the range it calls safe even with long exposure. At night it is a sleep issue rather than a hearing one.

Published health guidance is context. It is not a verdict on a recording from a phone.

Measuring with SOUNDTEST.PRO

The browser meter gives an estimate from the microphone in the phone or computer, not a reading from a certified sound level meter. The product's accuracy page describes a typical field spread of about ±1 to 5 dB after the built-in device baseline. Use it to compare times and keep a dated note.
