FAQ

Frequently asked questions about decibel levels

What dB level is considered quiet?

Background levels around 30 dB or below are considered quiet. A quiet bedroom at night sits near 25 to 30 dB, a quiet library near 35 to 40 dB, and a soft whisper at 1 meter is about 20 to 30 dB.

What dB level is safe for long-term exposure?

WHO environmental noise guidance treats long-term ambient exposure above roughly 55 dB daytime and 40 dB nighttime as potential sleep and cardiovascular health risks. OSHA sets 85 dB TWA as the workplace action level for mandatory hearing protection.

How loud is 70 dB compared to everyday sounds?

70 dB is roughly the noise level of a vacuum cleaner at 1 meter, a passenger car cruising at 10 meters, or normal television audio. It is loud enough to make sustained conversation harder but does not cause immediate hearing damage.

What dB level can cause hearing damage?

Sustained exposure above 85 dB over an 8-hour workday can cause gradual, permanent sensorineural hearing loss. Sound levels above 100 dB can cause damage in 15 minutes, while short-term impulse noise above 120–140 dB can cause immediate acoustic trauma.

How accurate is this dB reference chart?

The values shown are typical ranges compiled from WHO Environmental Noise Guidelines, NIOSH, OSHA, and published acoustic literature. Actual readings vary by distance, room acoustics, and microphone position. Use this chart to interpret browser dB readings, not as a substitute for certified Type 1 or Type 2 meters.

Can I use these values to file a noise complaint?

Reference values help establish context and corroborate your documentation, but statutory enforcement requires certified equipment and compliance with local municipal codes. SOUNDTEST.PRO provides civilian evidence records to support discussions with property managers or local authorities.

SOUNDTEST.PRO is a civilian acoustic evidence and documentation aid, not a certified Class 1/2 sound level meter. Verify formal legal or statutory claims with calibrated equipment.
