How loud is 30 dB?

30 dB is very quiet. It is roughly the level of a soft whisper a short distance away, a quiet rural night with no wind, or a well-insulated bedroom late at night with appliances off. Most people notice it as near-silence: small sounds such as a clock or your own breathing stand out.

What about 30 dB sounds like

Few homes in a city actually stay this quiet. A refrigerator, ventilation, plumbing, or distant traffic usually pushes indoor levels above 30 dB. Because decibels are logarithmic, 30 dB carries about one tenth of the sound energy of 40 dB, and most listeners hear a 10 dB drop as about half as loud. These everyday comparisons are approximate, not laboratory measurements.

30 dB matters mainly for sleep. The WHO 1999 Guidelines for Community Noise give an indoor bedroom guideline of 30 dB LAeq for continuous noise, and 45 dB LAmax for single events, as a sleep-disturbance reference. If a bedroom reads well above 30 dB at night, a steady source such as a fan, an air conditioner, or road traffic may be the reason. That reading is still an estimate from the microphone you used.

Phone and laptop microphones often have their own noise in very quiet rooms, so a browser meter may read higher than the room. Treat a very low reading as an estimate.

Hearing

30 dB is not a hearing-damage level. NIDCD says sounds at or below 70 dBA are unlikely to cause hearing loss even after long exposure. The relevant concern here is sleep, not hearing.

Published health guidance is context. It is not a verdict on a recording from a phone.
