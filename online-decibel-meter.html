What you can measure

  • A, C, and Z weighting. A-weighting (dBA) is the usual choice for everyday noise. C-weighting (dBC) keeps more bass, which helps with engines and thump. Z-weighting is unweighted. Fast (125 ms) and Slow (1 s) time response are both in the meter.
  • LAeq, L10, and L90. LAeq is the equivalent level over the measurement. L10 is the level exceeded about 10% of the time. L90 is the level exceeded about 90% of the time, closer to the background. The meter also computes L5, L50, and L95.
  • A record you can keep. You can start a recording in the browser. Photos can carry the live level and the time, and a place note when you allow location. A watermarked PDF preview is free. Removing that watermark is paid. A CSV of the session figures can be downloaded from the meter. Current prices are on the home page pricing table.

How accurate is a browser decibel meter?

Be realistic about a phone or laptop microphone. Built-in mics, automatic gain, and the operating system's processing differ by device, so a browser reading is an estimate, not a measurement from a certified sound level meter. The accuracy page and the meter's calibration screen describe a typical spread of about ±1 to 5 dB after the built-in device baseline. That is the product's stated field range. Very quiet rooms and very loud sources can sit outside what a phone microphone handles well.

Use it to spot trends, compare times of day, and keep a dated supporting note. If you need an official assessment for enforcement, a workplace rule, or a dispute that depends on the number itself, use a professional meter operated the way that rule requires.

How to get a useful reading

  1. Open the meter and allow microphone access.
  2. Choose A-weighting for general noise, or C-weighting for bass and low rumble.
  3. Hold the phone still, away from your body and not covered, pointed toward the source.
  4. Measure for at least a few minutes and note LAeq, not only the peak.
  5. Save a watermarked PDF preview, or a CSV, if you want a file. A clean PDF is a paid extra.

Online decibel meter questions

Is this online decibel meter free?

Live A, C, and Z weighting, a recording you start yourself, the time, optional location, and a watermarked PDF preview do not need a subscription. Removing the watermark is paid. Prices are on the home page. CSV export of the session figures is available from the meter.

How accurate is a phone or browser decibel meter?

It gives an estimate. Readings depend on the microphone and the audio processing. The accuracy page describes a typical spread of about ±1 to 5 dB after the device baseline. It is not a certified instrument.

Can I use the readings in a noise complaint?

You can use them as a supporting record when talking with a neighbor, a landlord, or a property manager, or when you file a complaint. Whether they are accepted is up to whoever handles it. Formal assessments use professional sound level meters.

Do I need to install an app?

No. It runs in a current mobile or desktop browser. You only need to allow microphone access.

What is the difference between dBA, dBC, and dBZ?

dBA is weighted to approximate human hearing and is the usual scale for noise limits. dBC keeps more low-frequency sound, which is useful for bass and machinery. dBZ is unweighted. The meter offers all three.
