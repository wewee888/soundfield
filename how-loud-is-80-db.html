How loud is 80 dB?

80 dB is loud. Approximate comparisons include standing near heavy traffic, a garbage disposal or blender, a loud alarm clock nearby, or a noisy work floor. NIDCD lists lawn mowers at about 80–100 dBA and motorcycles at about 80–110 dBA, so 80 dB sits at the bottom of those ranges, not at every mower or bike.

What about 80 dB sounds like

You usually need to raise your voice somewhat to talk at about 80 dB. Compared with 70 dB, it has about ten times the sound energy and is usually heard as about twice as loud.

80 dB is the reference in WHO's safe-listening advice: up to about 40 hours a week at 80 dB, and about four hours a week at 90 dB. That guidance is aimed at music through headphones and other leisure noise. If a commute, a gym, or a workplace often sits around 80 dB, the useful number is LAeq over the whole session, not one peak.

Use A-weighting for this kind of comparison. C-weighting keeps more bass and will not match an A-weighted limit.

Hearing

80 dB is below the NIOSH recommended exposure limit of 85 dBA over 8 hours. WHO still advises limiting leisure listening at 80 dB to about 40 hours a week. Long or repeated exposure at 85 dBA and above is where NIDCD says hearing loss becomes a risk.

Published health guidance is context. It is not a verdict on a recording from a phone.

Measuring with SOUNDTEST.PRO

The browser meter gives an estimate from the microphone in the phone or computer, not a reading from a certified sound level meter. The product's accuracy page describes a typical field spread of about ±1 to 5 dB after the built-in device baseline. Use it to compare times and keep a dated note. A watermarked PDF preview is included; removing the watermark is paid. CSV export of the session figures is available from the meter. For an official, workplace, or enforcement measurement, use a professional meter and the method that rule names.
