FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Are phone recordings legal evidence in a rental dispute?

In most regions you may record audio in your own home for personal documentation. Admissibility in a formal hearing depends on local law and the type of dispute; small-claims and tenancy tribunals often accept well-organized documentation even if it is not from a certified instrument. SOUNDTEST.PRO records are documentation-grade estimates, not certified measurements; pair them with photos and a written timeline for the strongest case.

How many noise incidents do I need to document for a rental dispute?

Aim for at least 6 to 10 documented events across several weeks, ideally with timestamps, dB trends, and notes about duration and impact. A clear, repeated pattern is far more persuasive than a single loud night. Keep your records in one exportable PDF so a landlord, mediator, or judge can scan them in minutes.

Does soundtest evidence hold up in small claims court?

SOUNDTEST.PRO records are treated as supporting documentation, not certified measurements. They are useful for establishing a pattern of disturbance, but a final ruling will weigh calibrated measurements, witness statements, lease terms, and local ordinance. Bring your records alongside any other evidence you have; do not rely on them alone.

Should I notify my landlord before recording noise?

You do not usually need to inform your landlord before documenting in your own home, but sending a brief written complaint that includes your records is the right next step. A paper trail dated before any escalation protects you if the dispute later goes to mediation or court. SOUNDTEST.PRO exports PDF reports that are easy to attach to an email.

SOUNDTEST.PRO is a documentation-grade estimate tool, not a certified sound level meter. Verify any formal claim with calibrated equipment and qualified measurement procedures.
