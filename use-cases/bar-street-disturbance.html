Most municipal codes evaluate commercial noise over a 10 to 15-minute equivalent continuous sound level (LAeq). Run a full session rather than taking a 3-second reading.

Practical Advice

How to make municipal code enforcement take immediate action

City authorities receive hundreds of noise complaints every weekend. Here is how to ensure yours receives priority inspection.

🪟
Rule 1: Record both open and closed window benchmarks First record 5 minutes with windows closed (showing indoor habitability breach), then 5 minutes with window cracked open (showing outdoor direct venue emission).
📜
Rule 2: Cite the venue's specific permit and closing hour Look up the establishment's liquor license closing time. If music is blasting past the permitted patio hour (e.g. 23:00), highlight the exact timestamp in your dossier.
🤝
Rule 3: Coordinate with adjacent neighbors for joint records Have 2–3 neighbors in different units run SOUNDTEST.PRO on the same night. Synchronized multi-unit records eliminate any defense that one tenant is "hyper-sensitive."
⚖️
Rule 4: Understand the civilian evidence boundary SOUNDTEST.PRO provides supporting documentation to initiate formal enforcement. Official permit revocations ultimately rely on certified municipal field inspectors.
FAQ

Frequently asked questions about venue and street noise

Most cities set venue indoor music limits between 85 and 100 dB inside, with outdoor or open-window limits of 55 to 70 dB at the property line after 10 or 11 PM. Rules differ sharply by jurisdiction, license type, and time of day. Document the dB level, time, and your location relative to the venue, then check your local entertainment or noise ordinance.
Start with the venue manager or security during the event so they can act in real time. For repeated issues, file a complaint with your city's licensing board, code enforcement, or non-emergency police line. Include timestamps, dB readings, and a short note about whether the issue is one-off or ongoing. SOUNDTEST.PRO reports can be exported as PDF for formal complaints.
In most jurisdictions you can record audio inside your own residence for personal documentation. Laws vary for sharing or publishing that recording, and a few regions require one-party or two-party consent. Use the recordings as supporting evidence; do not claim they are calibrated or legally definitive. SOUNDTEST.PRO records are documentation-grade estimates, not certified measurements.
